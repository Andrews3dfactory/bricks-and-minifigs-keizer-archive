# Witness Statements
**Bricks & Minifigs Keizer  Mansell Collection Consignment Dispute**

> **PRIVACY NOTICE:** The 83-year-old father must NOT be contacted under any circumstances. He is unaware of the full situation and contact could seriously impact his health. ALL inquiries go through Bryan Mansell exclusively.

---

## Primary Witness  Bryan Mansell

**Role:** Son, co-collector, sole point of contact
**Items Consigned:** 780+ LEGO Star Wars sets; 1,200+ minifigures  many new-in-box
**Collection Value:** 15 years of curated collecting; intended to fund grandchildren's college education
**Current Status:** Unrecovered  believed sold without authorization

---

## The Collection  Background

> "My father who is 83 years old and myself have been building a massive Star Wars LEGO collection over the course of the last 15 years. LEGO was a toy we shared when I was a kid, and he wanted to share it with his grandchildren. He chose LEGO as an investment and began purchasing sets and figures to be kept new and in box, so that one day they could be sold to help pay for the grandkid's college education. In total we had collected over 780 sets and 1200 minifigures."

- 15 years of deliberate father-son collecting
- All items kept new-in-box as a long-term investment
- Purpose: grandchildren's college education fund
- One of the largest LEGO Star Wars consignment collections in the Pacific Northwest

---

## Consignment Begins  October 2023

> "In October of 2023, I took this collection into the Keizer, OR. Bricks and Minifigs location to consign with the store and create a contract for them to sell it over the next year or so. After meeting with the owner, Chrystal Law (Now Chrystal Gorman after getting married this year), we gave her close to 40 sets to take with her and sell at the comic con in Portland as sort of a test run. The reactions were great, and the sales went well so we felt it was a good fit to consign with her."

- Test run of ~40 sets at Portland Comic Con  strong sales, enthusiastic community response
- Full written consignment contract executed with Chrystal Law (now Gorman)
- Full collection transported to the Salem/Keizer area

> "After working a contract out and transporting all the sets down to the Salem/Keizer area, we had a big unveiling that was covered in the local press, social media posts, and a line of people out the door to see this once in a lifetime collection. You can find plenty of posts on social media still regarding this."

- Public unveiling covered by local press
- Social media posts about the event remain publicly accessible
- Line of people out the door  a documented, publicly witnessed event

## The Corporate Seizure

> "The Bricks and Minifigs Corporation had some sort of disagreement with the store franchise owner and decided to come in and seize the store and all of the merchandise. The problem is, they seized my collection which did not belong to them at all. They were told about this collection and never reached out to us for over a week."

- BAM corporate physically seized the Keizer store and all its contents
- The Mansell collection was consigned property  the family owned it, not the store
- Corporate was informed the collection existed and chose not to contact Bryan for over a week
- During that week: identifying stickers and tags were removed from collection items
- During that week: items continued to be sold without the Mansells' knowledge or consent

## The Smoking Gun  Security Camera Footage

> "We have included store security cam footage with volume where you can literally hear Chrystal saying I have not been paid yet for this month's sets, and Brandon turns his phone on speaker so you can hear corporate say, 'Well Chrystal, when it comes to the Mansell consignment, that is a deal with the business, and since Brandon will be taking the business over, he will be taking over the consignment as well.'"

This recording proves:

1. BAM corporate knew the Mansell consignment existed **by name**
2. Corporate made a unilateral decision about it **without contacting the Mansells**
3. Brandon Best was involved in these discussions **before** claiming he had never heard of the collection
4. Chrystal confirmed on camera she had **not been paid** for October 2024  the payment breach predates the seizure

Additionally: Brandon is captured on the same footage **receiving the store keys from Chrystal in person**  directly contradicting his later claim he had never met her before.
4. Chrystal confirmed on camera she had **not been paid** for October 2024

## The Confrontation  Four Contradictory Stories

Bryan drove to the store with: signed contract, complete inventory lists, all receipts, and the security footage.

**Story 1  "Never heard of it"**
> "When I finally became aware of the situation and let Brandon know, his first response, and lie, was to play dumb and say he had never heard about it."

*Directly contradicted by: the security footage in which corporate names the Mansell consignment and Brandon is present for the discussion.*

**Story 2  "No way to prove they're yours"**
> "He continued to lie and say they were unaware of any collection... Then he changed his story and claimed I had no way of proving the sets are ours, and they considered the matter closed."

*Directly contradicted by: inventory lists, monthly receipts, the signed contract, and the footage showing Brandon receiving keys from Chrystal in person  contradicting his claim of never having met her.*

**Story 3  "Chrystal stole everything"**
Brandon claimed Chrystal had stolen everything and left the store empty.

*Directly contradicted by: the back room inspection Brandon refused to allow.*

**Story 4  "We'll keep selling it after we deal with Chrystal"**
> "Then the story changed again when his corporate partner came out from the back room and tried to say we would love to keep selling the stuff for you, but first we need to go after Chrystal for the money she owes the corporation, which has nothing to do with us or our collection."

**The Final Word:**
> "I asked him if that was really the way he wanted to handle it, and the owner stood there, crossed his arms and with a smirk said, 'Yup! Take us to court.'"

- Police were called to the scene that night
- Store refused to allow Bryan or police to view the back room
- If nothing was there, opening the door would have resolved everything immediately
- The refusal is itself strong evidence the collection was present

## The $60,000 Barrier

> "At this point we reached out to an attorney who told us it would unfortunately cost a minimum $60k to put an injunction on the store and a cease and desist until they could go back and produce all receipts for the last 6 months as well as proof of purchase for any items (which they would not have been able to since they stole them from us). That amount is literally almost the entire amount our percentage would have been. So, we could spend all this money winning this battle, only to lose the war."

- $60,000+ minimum to pursue a court injunction
- Nearly equals the total value of the Mansell family's share of the collection
- BAM corporate calculated the family could not afford to fight back
- The "Take us to court" smirk reflects this deliberate economic strategy

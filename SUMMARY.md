# Case Summary
**Bricks & Minifigs Keizer  Mansell Collection Consignment Dispute**

---

## The Short Version

An 83-year-old Oregon grandfather and his son, **Bryan Mansell**, spent 15 years building a LEGO Star Wars collection of over **780 sets and 1,200 minifigures**  new-in-box, intended to fund their grandchildren's college education.

In October 2023, they consigned the collection to the Bricks & Minifigs (BAM) franchise store in Keizer, Oregon, under a written contract with store owner **Chrystal Law (now Chrystal Gorman)**. For a year, everything worked: monthly payments, itemized receipts, a thriving consignment relationship.

In November 2024, BAM corporate seized the Keizer store. They took the entire collection with it  property they did not own and had no right to take. Security camera footage captured corporate leadership **acknowledging the Mansell consignment by name** and unilaterally declaring the new operator, **Brandon Best**, would "take it over"  without ever contacting the Mansells.

By the time Bryan learned what had happened, identifying labels had been removed from his collection and items were already being sold. When he drove to the store with contracts, receipts, and the security footage, he was met with four different versions of "we don't know what you're talking about"  and ultimately told, with a smirk:

> *"Yup! Take us to court."

Police declined to take criminal action. Attorneys quoted a minimum **$60,000** to pursue an injunction  nearly the value of the Mansells' share of the collection. CEO **Ammon** promised police he would help locate and return the items, then never did. Corporate declared the matter "closed."

This is the full archive of that case.

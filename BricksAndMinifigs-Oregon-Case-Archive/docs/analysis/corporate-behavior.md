# Corporate Behavior Analysis  
*An evidence‑based examination of Bricks & Minifigs corporate actions during and after the Keizer store seizure*

This document analyzes the behavior, statements, and decisions made by Bricks & Minifigs (BAM) corporate during the events surrounding the Keizer, Oregon consignment case.  
It does not assign guilt or make legal conclusions.  
Instead, it presents a structured, neutral breakdown of how corporate actions aligned — or conflicted — with documented evidence, franchise norms, and standard consignment practices.

---

# 🧱 1. Corporate Involvement in the Keizer Store Seizure

## 1.1. Corporate Seized the Store from the Franchise Owner  
Evidence shows that BAM corporate took control of the Keizer location from franchise owner **Chrystal Law (now Chrystal Gorman)** in November 2024.

During this takeover:

- Corporate staff entered the store  
- Chrystal was removed from her position  
- Inventory and operations were transferred to a new operator  
- The store reopened under corporate‑installed management  

This action is consistent with corporate enforcement of franchise agreements — but it created complications regarding consigned property.

---

## 1.2. Corporate Acknowledged the Consignment Agreement  
In recorded audio captured during the takeover, a corporate representative stated:

> “When it comes to the Mansell consignment, that is a deal with the business, and since Brandon will be taking the business over, he will be taking over the consignment as well.”

This acknowledgment is significant because it:

- Confirms corporate was aware of the consignment  
- Confirms corporate recognized the agreement as valid  
- Assigns responsibility to the new operator, **Brandon Best**  

This statement conflicts with later corporate behavior.

---

# 🧱 2. Corporate Communication Patterns

## 2.1. Initial Cooperation  
Immediately after the takeover, corporate representatives:

- Spoke directly with the consignor  
- Acknowledged the consignment agreement  
- Indicated they would review inventory  
- Suggested the matter would be resolved  

This early communication appeared cooperative.

---

## 2.2. Sudden Shift in Tone  
Shortly after the takeover, corporate communication changed:

- Emails went unanswered  
- Phone calls were not returned  
- Promised inventory reviews never occurred  
- Corporate representatives became dismissive  

Eventually, corporate stated they considered the matter **“closed”**, despite unresolved ownership issues.

---

## 2.3. Conflicting Statements  
Corporate representatives made statements that contradicted:

- Their own earlier statements  
- Statements made by the new operator  
- Evidence documented in receipts, contracts, and footage  

Examples include:

- Acknowledging the consignment → later denying responsibility  
- Promising an inventory review → never conducting one  
- Claiming the matter was resolved → while items remained missing  

These inconsistencies are a key focus of this analysis.

---

# 🧱 3. Corporate Oversight and Responsibility

## 3.1. Responsibility During a Franchise Seizure  
When corporate seizes a store, they assume control of:

- Inventory  
- Operations  
- Customer accounts  
- Store assets  

The legal question is whether they also assume responsibility for:

- Consigned property  
- Outstanding agreements  
- Items not owned by the franchise  

Corporate’s recorded statement suggests they did.

---

## 3.2. Lack of Inventory Segregation  
During the takeover, corporate did not:

- Separate consigned items from store inventory  
- Document which items belonged to the consignor  
- Secure the collection  
- Prevent continued sales of consigned items  

This lack of segregation is a recurring issue in other BAM incidents.

---

## 3.3. Delegation to the New Operator  
Corporate installed **Brandon Best** as the new operator.  
They stated he would take over the consignment agreement.

However:

- Brandon denied knowledge of the consignment  
- Brandon refused to return items  
- Brandon removed identifying labels  
- Brandon continued selling consigned items  
- Brandon told the consignor to “take us to court”  

Corporate did not intervene to correct or address these actions.

---

# 🧱 4. Corporate Behavior Compared to Franchise Norms

## 4.1. Standard Franchise Practice  
In most franchise systems, corporate is expected to:

- Protect customer property  
- Maintain accurate inventory  
- Honor existing agreements  
- Provide clear communication  
- Resolve disputes professionally  

The documented behavior in this case deviates from these norms.

---

## 4.2. Similar Incidents in Other Locations  
Other Oregon franchisees — including **Canby** — reported:

- Corporate seizures  
- Disputed inventory handling  
- Loss of consigned or privately owned items  
- Poor communication  
- Conflicting statements  

These parallels suggest systemic issues rather than isolated incidents.

---

# 🧱 5. Potential Motivations and Incentives  
*(Neutral analysis — not an accusation)*

Several factors may have influenced corporate behavior:

### ✔️ Financial Incentives  
Seizing inventory — including consigned items — may reduce losses during a franchise takeover.

### ✔️ Liability Avoidance  
Denying responsibility may reduce corporate exposure to legal claims.

### ✔️ Operational Pressure  
Corporate may prioritize reopening the store quickly over resolving consignment disputes.

### ✔️ Documentation Gaps  
Inconsistent consignment practices across stores create confusion during transitions.

These factors do not justify the behavior — they help explain it.

# 🧱 6. Impact of Corporate Behavior

Corporate actions had significant consequences:

### ✔️ For the Consignor  
- Loss of a 15‑year family collection  
- Emotional distress  
- Financial loss  
- Legal costs too high to pursue  

### ✔️ For the Community  
- Loss of trust in the franchise  
- Concern among consignors  
- Damage to the local LEGO community  

### ✔️ For the Franchise Network  
- Public scrutiny  
- Pattern documentation  
- Potential legal exposure  

---

# 🧱 7. Summary of Corporate Behavior Issues

This analysis identifies several key issues:

- Acknowledgment of the consignment agreement  
- Failure to segregate consigned property  
- Conflicting statements  
- Lack of follow‑through  
- Delegation to an operator who denied responsibility  
- Dismissal of the matter despite unresolved ownership  
- Patterns consistent with other Oregon incidents  

These issues do not prove wrongdoing.  
They highlight areas where corporate behavior conflicts with:

- Standard franchise practices  
- Consignment norms  
- Reasonable expectations of transparency

# Witness Statement Summaries  
*Structured summaries of firsthand accounts related to the Bricks & Minifigs Keizer consignment case*

This document contains **summaries** of firsthand accounts from individuals connected to the Bricks & Minifigs (BAM) Keizer store, the consignment process, and the events surrounding the corporate seizure.  
These statements are anonymized unless the contributor has explicitly agreed to be identified.

The purpose of this file is to provide investigators, journalists, and community members with a clear understanding of what witnesses observed — without revealing private personal information.

These are **summaries**, not verbatim transcripts.

---

# 🧱 1. Former Employee Statements

## 1.1. Employee A — Keizer Store Staff (2023–2024)
**Summary:**  
This employee reported that the consigned LEGO Star Wars collection was:

- Brought in gradually over multiple visits  
- Logged and labeled by the store  
- Displayed prominently in the front of the store  
- A major draw for customers  
- Sold regularly with receipts issued to the consignor  

They stated that **Chrystal Law (Gorman)** handled all consignment paperwork and payouts.

After the corporate takeover, the employee observed:

- Labels removed from many consigned sets  
- Boxes moved into the back room  
- Confusion among staff about which items belonged to the store  
- Corporate staff instructing employees not to discuss the situation  

The employee described the environment as “chaotic” and “unorganized.”

---

## 1.2. Employee B — Present During Corporate Takeover
**Summary:**  
This employee witnessed the moment corporate arrived to seize the store.  
They reported:

- Corporate representatives entered with little warning  
- Chrystal was removed from the premises  
- Inventory was boxed, moved, or reorganized  
- No clear process for separating consigned items from store inventory  

They also confirmed hearing corporate acknowledge the consignment agreement on speakerphone.

---

# 🧱 2. Customer Statements

## 2.1. Customer A — Regular Shopper
**Summary:**  
This customer recalled:

- Seeing the consigned LEGO collection on display for months  
- Speaking with Chrystal about the collection’s size and value  
- Observing price tags and labels identifying items as consigned  
- Noticing that after the takeover, many of those items disappeared  

They stated that the collection was “one of the biggest attractions in the store.”

---

## 2.2. Customer B — Present During a Dispute
**Summary:**  
This customer was in the store during one of the conversations between the consignor and new operator **Brandon Best**.

They reported hearing Brandon:

- Deny knowledge of the consignment  
- Claim the store was “empty” when he took over  
- Tell the consignor to “take us to court”  

The customer described the interaction as “hostile” and “dismissive.”

---

# 🧱 3. Franchisee Statements (Other Locations)

## 3.1. Former Oregon Franchisee — Canby Location
**Summary:**  
This franchisee reported experiencing:

- A sudden corporate seizure  
- Loss of inventory  
- Disputed ownership of items  
- Poor communication from corporate  
- Financial strain caused by the takeover  

They stated that the Keizer incident “sounds exactly like what happened to us.”

---

## 3.2. Franchisee C — Out‑of‑State Location
**Summary:**  
This franchisee described:

- Corporate taking aggressive action during disputes  
- Inventory being seized without clear documentation  
- Consigned items being mixed with store inventory  
- Corporate refusing to mediate customer complaints  

They emphasized that consignment practices vary widely across the franchise network.

---

# 🧱 4. Consignor Statements (Keizer Case)

## 4.1. Primary Consignor — Family Member
**Summary:**  
The consignor reported:

- A 15‑year effort to build the LEGO collection  
- A formal consignment agreement with Chrystal  
- Monthly payouts and receipts for over a year  
- A sudden loss of communication in November 2024  
- Discovering the store had been seized by corporate  
- Being denied access to the back room  
- Seeing labels removed from consigned items  
- Being told conflicting stories by Brandon Best  
- Being told to “take us to court”  

They provided extensive documentation, including:

- Contracts  
- Receipts  
- Inventory lists  
- Photos  
- Video footage  
- Audio recordings  

---

# 🧱 5. Corporate Statements (Summarized)

## 5.1. Corporate Representative — Recorded Audio
**Summary:**  
In a recorded call during the takeover, a corporate representative stated:

> “Brandon will be taking over the consignment as well.”

This statement:

- Acknowledges the consignment  
- Assigns responsibility to the new operator  
- Conflicts with later corporate communication  

---

## 5.2. Corporate Follow‑Up Communications
**Summary:**  
Corporate later:

- Stopped responding to emails  
- Did not conduct the promised inventory review  
- Declared the matter “closed”  
- Denied responsibility for the consigned items  

These statements conflict with the earlier recorded acknowledgment.

# 🧱 6. Themes Across Witness Accounts

Across all statements, several consistent themes emerge:

### ✔️ The collection was publicly displayed and widely recognized  
### ✔️ The consignment agreement was known to staff and customers  
### ✔️ Labels were removed after the corporate takeover  
### ✔️ Inventory was reorganized without documentation  
### ✔️ Corporate acknowledged the consignment, then denied responsibility  
### ✔️ The new operator provided conflicting explanations  
### ✔️ Witnesses observed confusion, disorganization, and inconsistent communication  

These themes do not prove wrongdoing.  
They highlight areas where witness accounts align with documented evidence.

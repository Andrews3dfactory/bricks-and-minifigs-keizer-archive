# Timeline of Events  
*Bricks & Minifigs Keizer, Oregon Consignment Case*

This timeline presents a clear, factual, and fully documented sequence of events surrounding the consignment, seizure, and disputed handling of a large LEGO Star Wars collection at the Bricks & Minifigs (BAM) franchise store in Keizer, Oregon. It blends investigative clarity with the human context of a family who spent 15 years building a collection meant to inspire future generations.

---

## 🧱 2008–2023 — The Collection Is Built

- Over a span of **15 years**, an Oregon grandfather and his son build a massive LEGO Star Wars collection.
- The collection grows to **780+ sets** and **1,200+ minifigures**, many sealed and investment‑grade.
- The grandfather, age 83, intends the collection to help fund his grandchildren’s future education.
- LEGO becomes a shared family tradition, connecting three generations.

---

## 🧱 Mid–2023 — Initial Contact with Bricks & Minifigs Keizer

- The family approaches the **Bricks & Minifigs Keizer, Oregon** store about consigning the collection.
- Store owner **Chrystal Law (later Chrystal Gorman)** expresses interest.
- A “test run” is arranged: approximately **40 sets** are provided for sale at a Portland comic convention.
- Sales are strong, community response is enthusiastic, and both parties agree to proceed.

---

## 🧱 Late 2023 — Full Consignment Begins

- The entire collection is transported to the Keizer store.
- A formal **consignment contract** is signed.
- The store hosts a **public unveiling event**, drawing:
  - Local news coverage  
  - Social media attention  
  - A line of customers out the door  
- The collection becomes a local LEGO community highlight.

---

## 🧱 2023–2024 — Consignment Operates Smoothly

- On the **15th of each month**, the consignor visits the store to receive:
  - A stack of receipts  
  - A payout check  
  - A summary of sets and minifigures sold  
- All documentation is retained, including:
  - Receipts  
  - Inventory lists  
  - Payment records  
- The arrangement appears stable and successful.

---

## ⚠️ November 2024 — Communication Breaks Down

- **November 14–18, 2024:** The consignor attempts to contact Chrystal with no response.
- Phone calls to the store reveal:
  - Chrystal is “no longer with the company.”
  - A new operator, **Brandon Best**, has taken over.
  - Bricks & Minifigs corporate has **seized the store** during a dispute with the franchise owner.

### Critical Issue:
The family’s consigned collection — **still legally theirs** — is seized along with store inventory.

---

## ⚠️ Late November 2024 — Discovery of the Seizure

- The consignor visits the store in person with:
  - Contracts  
  - Inventory documentation  
  - Security footage  
  - Proof of prior sales  
- Security footage captures:
  - Chrystal stating she has not yet been paid for the month’s consignment sales.
  - Brandon placing corporate on speakerphone.
  - Corporate stating:  
    **“When it comes to the Mansell consignment, that is a deal with the business, and since Brandon will be taking the business over, he will be taking over the consignment as well.”**

### Store Response:
Brandon Best allegedly:

- Denies knowing about the consignment  
- Claims the consignor cannot prove ownership  
- Changes his story multiple times:
  - “Chrystal stole everything.”  
  - “The store was empty.”  
  - “We’ll sell the items for you, but we need to go after Chrystal first.”  
- Refuses to allow access to the back room  
- Smirks and says:  
  **“Yup! Take us to court.”**

---

## 🚨 Police Involvement

- Police are called to the store.
- Officers acknowledge potential criminal elements but ultimately classify it as a **civil matter**.
- Despite visible evidence and identifiable sets, no immediate action is taken.
- The consignor is advised to pursue civil litigation.

---

## ⚖️ Late 2024 — Legal Attempts

- Multiple attorneys are contacted.
- Cease‑and‑desist letters and termination notices are drafted and sent.
- Bricks & Minifigs corporate:
  - Ignores or dismisses communications  
  - Claims they will “review inventory” but never do  
  - Later states they consider the matter **“closed”**  

### Legal Cost Barrier:
- Attorneys estimate **$60,000+** to pursue an injunction and full litigation.
- This amount is nearly equal to the consignor’s expected share of the collection’s value.
- Corporate appears to rely on the cost barrier to deter action.

---

## 🧩 Pattern Emerges

- Other Oregon BAM franchisees, including **Canby**, report similar issues involving:
  - Corporate store seizures  
  - Disputed inventory handling  
  - Loss of consigned or privately owned items  
- A broader pattern of behavior begins to surface.

---

## 📅 Ongoing — Public Documentation and Investigation

- With legal avenues financially unrealistic, the family and investigators turn to:
  - Public documentation  
  - Social media awareness  
  - Community outreach  
  - Independent investigation  
- This repository is created to:
  - Preserve evidence  
  - Provide transparency  
  - Support journalists and investigators  
  - Protect future consignors and collectors  

---

## 🧱 Why This Timeline Matters

This case is more than a business dispute.  
It represents:

- A family’s lost legacy  
- A breach of community trust  
- A potential pattern of corporate misconduct  
- A warning to consignors and collectors nationwide  

By documenting the events clearly and publicly, this timeline ensures the story cannot be buried — and that the LEGO community has the information it needs to protect itself.

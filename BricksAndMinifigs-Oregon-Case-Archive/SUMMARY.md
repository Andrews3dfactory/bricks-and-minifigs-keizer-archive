# Summary of the Bricks & Minifigs Keizer, Oregon Consignment Incident  
*A comprehensive overview of the events, evidence, and community impact*

In 2023, an 83‑year‑old Oregon grandfather and his son consigned one of the largest privately held LEGO Star Wars collections in the Pacific Northwest to the Bricks & Minifigs (BAM) franchise store in Keizer, Oregon. The collection — more than **780 LEGO sets** and **1,200 minifigures**, many new‑in‑box — represented **15 years of shared collecting**, a family tradition intended to help fund future college expenses for the grandchildren.

For roughly a year, the consignment arrangement functioned smoothly. The store’s then‑owner, **Chrystal Law (now Chrystal Gorman)**, sold items, issued receipts, and provided monthly payouts. The collection was even featured in local media, drawing crowds and generating excitement within the LEGO community.

Everything changed in **November 2024**, when Bricks & Minifigs corporate seized the Keizer store during a dispute with the franchise owner. During this takeover, the family’s consigned collection — which they still legally owned — was seized along with store inventory. Corporate leadership was recorded acknowledging the consignment agreement and stating that the new operator, **Brandon Best**, would assume responsibility for it.

Despite this, the new store operator allegedly:

- Removed identifying labels from consigned items  
- Continued selling the collection without authorization  
- Denied knowledge of the consignment  
- Refused to allow inspection of the back room  
- Provided multiple conflicting explanations  
- Told the consignor to “take us to court”  

Police were contacted but treated the matter as a civil dispute, despite indications of potential criminal activity. Attorneys estimated that pursuing an injunction and full litigation could cost **$60,000 or more**, making legal action financially prohibitive.

Attempts to resolve the issue with Bricks & Minifigs corporate — including cease‑and‑desist letters, termination notices, and direct communication — were ignored or dismissed. Corporate representatives reportedly stated they considered the matter “closed,” despite the unresolved ownership of hundreds of consigned items.

This incident is not isolated. Other Oregon franchisees, including the Canby location, have reported similar issues involving corporate seizures and disputed inventory handling.

This repository exists to:

- Preserve evidence  
- Document the timeline  
- Support investigative efforts  
- Provide transparency for the LEGO community  
- Highlight potential systemic issues within the franchise network  

The case represents more than a business dispute — it reflects the loss of a family legacy, the betrayal of community trust, and the vulnerability of consignors who rely on franchise systems to act in good faith.

This archive is a public resource for investigators, journalists, collectors, and anyone seeking to understand what happened inside the Keizer Bricks & Minifigs store and why it matters to the broader LEGO community.

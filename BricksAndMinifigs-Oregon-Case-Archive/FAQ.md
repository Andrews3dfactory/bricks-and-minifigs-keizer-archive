# Frequently Asked Questions  
*Bricks & Minifigs Keizer, Oregon Consignment Case*

This FAQ provides clear, accessible answers to the most common questions surrounding the consignment dispute at the Bricks & Minifigs (BAM) store in Keizer, Oregon. It blends investigative clarity with the community‑centered context that makes this case matter to LEGO fans, collectors, and consignors everywhere.

---

## ❓ What is this case about?

This case involves a large LEGO Star Wars collection — more than **780 sets** and **1,200 minifigures** — consigned to the Bricks & Minifigs Keizer store. After a corporate seizure of the franchise in November 2024, the collection was allegedly taken, sold, or withheld without authorization, despite clear documentation proving ownership.

---

## ❓ Who owned the LEGO collection?

The collection belonged to an **83‑year‑old Oregon grandfather** and his son. They spent 15 years building it together as a family legacy and future investment for the grandchildren’s education. Ownership was never transferred to Bricks & Minifigs.

---

## ❓ Who was the original store owner?

The Keizer store was originally owned and operated by **Chrystal Law (now Chrystal Gorman)**. She handled the consignment agreement, monthly payouts, and public display of the collection.

---

## ❓ What happened in November 2024?

Bricks & Minifigs corporate seized the Keizer store during a dispute with the franchise owner. During this takeover:

- The family’s consigned collection was seized along with store inventory.  
- Corporate leadership acknowledged the consignment agreement on recorded audio.  
- A new operator, **Brandon Best**, was installed to run the store.  

Despite this, the new operator allegedly denied knowledge of the consignment and refused to return the items.

---

All evidence is archived in the `docs/evidence/` folder of this repository.

---

## ❓ What did the new operator, Brandon Best, do?

According to documentation and witness accounts, Brandon Best:

- Removed identifying labels from consigned items  
- Continued selling the collection  
- Denied knowing about the consignment  
- Provided multiple conflicting explanations  
- Refused access to the back room  
- Told the consignor: **“Yup! Take us to court.”**

---

## ❓ What was Bricks & Minifigs corporate’s response?

Corporate representatives:

- Acknowledged the consignment agreement on recorded audio  
- Promised to review inventory but never followed through  
- Ignored cease‑and‑desist letters  
- Declared the matter “closed” despite unresolved ownership issues  

Other franchisees in Oregon have reported similar experiences during corporate seizures.

---

## ❓ Why didn’t the police intervene?

Police acknowledged that criminal activity *may* have occurred but ultimately classified the situation as a **civil dispute**, advising the family to pursue legal action instead.

---

## ❓ Why didn’t the family sue?

Attorneys estimated that pursuing an injunction and full litigation would cost **$60,000 or more** — nearly equal to the consignor’s expected share of the collection’s value. This financial barrier made litigation unrealistic.

---

## ❓ Is LEGO (the company) involved?

No. LEGO has stated that it has **no affiliation** with Bricks & Minifigs and does not oversee or regulate BAM franchise operations.

---

## ❓ Is this an isolated incident?

No. Other Oregon BAM franchisees — including the Canby location — have reported similar issues involving:

- Corporate store seizures  
- Disputed inventory handling  
- Loss of consigned or privately owned items  

This repository documents those patterns in `docs/analysis/franchise-patterns.md`.

---

## ❓ Why is this case important to the LEGO community?

Because it highlights:

- The vulnerability of consignors  
- The risks of franchise‑based resale systems  
- The importance of transparency and documentation  
- The impact of corporate actions on families and collectors  

The LEGO community thrives on trust, creativity, and shared passion. When that trust is broken, it affects everyone.
